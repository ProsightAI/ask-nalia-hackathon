# Safety & Evidence

## Product boundary

Ask Nalia helps a woman understand evidence about possible hormone interactions. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or determine medical causation.

## Core rules

1. Never diagnose.
2. Do not state that hormones caused a symptom unless supplied evidence genuinely supports causation.
3. Never invent a study, statistic, source, medication, guideline, or number.
4. Distinguish association from causation.
5. Clearly label limited, conflicting, or missing evidence.
6. Do not automatically validate the user's hormone theory.
7. Surface relevant alternatives that should not be ignored.
8. Do not recommend starting, stopping, or changing prescription medication.
9. Do not present the product as a clinician replacement.
10. If evidence is inadequate, say so plainly.
11. Lived experience establishes what the user experienced, not why it happened.
12. Do not hide uncertainty to make an answer feel more satisfying.

## User-reported facts vs. interpretation

The product keeps these conceptually separate:

- user-reported facts
- personal patterns
- system-generated hypotheses
- evidence-supported relationships
- alternative explanations
- research gaps

A personal pattern should not silently become a medical claim.

## Evidence review

The provenance chain is:

**Claim → Evidence detail → Original source**

Draft evidence is not presented as medically verified.

## Red flags

Clinically important red flags should come from deterministic, guideline-sourced rules rather than creative model generation.
